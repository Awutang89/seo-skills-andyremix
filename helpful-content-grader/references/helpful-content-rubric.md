# Helpful Content Grading Rubric

> **Source:** Google Search Central, *"Creating helpful, reliable, people-first content"*
> <https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content>
>
> This rubric is the authoritative scoring reference. Grade the submitted content against every
> question below. If SKILL.md and this file disagree, this file wins.

## How scoring works

- **32 questions** across 4 sections.
- Each question is **PASS** or **FAIL**. **PARTIAL** = half credit, use sparingly and say why.
- **N/A** only when a question genuinely cannot apply (e.g. Q4 when no outside sources are used;
  site-level questions when grading a draft with no site context). N/A questions are dropped and
  their weight is redistributed evenly among the remaining applicable questions **in the same
  section**, so each section keeps its intended share.

### Scoring modes

The final percentage is always out of 100. What changes between modes is how much each question
is worth.

**Mode A: Equal weight**

- Every question worth the same. Each PASS = 1 point, max 32.
- Overall = (points earned / applicable questions) x 100.

**Mode B: Section-weighted (default)**

Each section owns a fixed share of the 100 points; questions split their section's share equally.

| Section | Topic | Share | Per question |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Content and quality | **40%** | 3.33 pts each across 12 Qs |
| 2 | Expertise | **30%** | 7.5 pts each across 4 Qs |
| 3 | People-first | **20%** | 4 pts each across 5 Qs |
| 4 | Avoid search-engine-first | **10%** | 0.91 pts each across 11 Qs |

Overall = sum of points earned (PASS earns the question's full value, PARTIAL half, FAIL 0).

**Mode C: YMYL-adaptive**

For "Your Money or Your Life" topics (health, finance, safety, legal, major life decisions),
trust and expertise carry more weight:

| Section | Topic | Share |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Content and quality | **30%** |
| 2 | Expertise | **40%** |
| 3 | People-first | **20%** |
| 4 | Avoid search-engine-first | **10%** |

Same math as Mode B with these weights. If the topic is clearly not YMYL, fall back to Mode B.

> In all modes: round the final percentage to a whole number and map it to the letter band below.
> Always state the mode and show per-section points so the math is transparent.

### Two scoring directions

Sections 1, 2, 3 are **positive** questions: a "yes" about the content is good, so yes = PASS.

Section 4 is **reverse-scored**: the questions describe warning signs. A "yes" means the content
shows the bad behavior.

- **PASS** = the warning sign is **absent** (honest answer is "no").
- **FAIL** = the warning sign is **present** (honest answer is "yes").

### Letter-grade band

| Grade | Range |
|---|---|
| A | 90-100 |
| B | 80-89 |
| C | 70-79 |
| D | 60-69 |
| F | below 60 |

## Section 1: Content and Quality (12 questions)

**Q1. Original information.** Does the content provide original information, reporting, research,
or analysis?
- PASS: first-hand data, original reporting, proprietary research, testing, or analysis not found
  by simply reading other pages.
- FAIL: entirely reassembled from existing sources with no original contribution.

**Q2. Comprehensive description.** Does the content provide a substantial, complete, or
comprehensive description of the topic?
- PASS: covers the topic thoroughly; a reader's main questions are answered without another page.
- FAIL: thin, surface-level, or leaves obvious gaps.

**Q3. Insightful analysis beyond the obvious.** Does the content provide insightful analysis or
interesting information that is beyond the obvious?
- PASS: non-obvious insight, interpretation, or detail a casual writer would miss.
- FAIL: only common-knowledge or obvious points.

**Q4. Adds value over sources.** If the content draws on other sources, does it avoid simply
copying or rewriting them, and instead provide substantial additional value and originality?
- PASS: synthesizes, adds commentary, evidence, or perspective beyond the source material.
  (N/A if the content cites no outside sources.)
- FAIL: reads as a paraphrase or rewrite of other pages.

**Q5. Descriptive title.** Does the main heading or page title provide a descriptive, helpful
summary of the content?
- PASS: title accurately and clearly describes what the page delivers.
- FAIL: vague, generic, or does not reflect the content.

**Q6. Non-exaggerated title.** Does the main heading or page title avoid exaggerating or being
shocking in nature?
- PASS: measured and accurate; no clickbait or hype.
- FAIL: exaggerates, sensationalizes, or uses shock value.

**Q7. Bookmark-worthy.** Is this the sort of page you'd want to bookmark, share with a friend, or
recommend?
- PASS: useful enough that a reasonable reader would save or share it.
- FAIL: forgettable; no reason to return or recommend.

**Q8. Reference-quality.** Would you expect to see this content in or referenced by a printed
magazine, encyclopedia, or book?
- PASS: meets a publishable, reference-grade standard of quality and credibility.
- FAIL: below the standard of an edited publication.

**Q9. Substantial value vs. competitors.** Does the content provide substantial value when
compared to other pages in search results?
- PASS: clearly more value than typical competing pages on the topic.
- FAIL: adds nothing beyond what comparable pages already provide.

**Q10. Spelling and style.** Does the content avoid spelling or stylistic issues?
- PASS: clean spelling, grammar, consistent style.
- FAIL: noticeable spelling, grammar, or stylistic problems.
- Note: Google phrases this as "Does the content HAVE any spelling or stylistic issues?" PASS
  here means NO issues.

**Q11. Produced with care.** Is the content produced well, rather than appearing sloppy or
hastily produced?
- PASS: well-structured, polished, evidently careful.
- FAIL: sloppy, rushed, or carelessly assembled.

**Q12. Not mass-produced.** Does the content avoid being mass-produced or outsourced to many
creators, or spread across a large network, such that pages don't get individual care?
- PASS: shows individual attention; not part of a churned content farm.
- FAIL: reads as mass-produced, templated, or low-attention network content.
- Site-level: N/A if no site context.

## Section 2: Expertise (4 questions)

**Q13. Trustworthy presentation.** Does the content present information in a way that makes you
want to trust it: clear sourcing, evidence of expertise, background about the author or site
(author page or About page)?
- PASS: sourcing and/or author/site background present and credible.
- FAIL: no sourcing, no author/site context, little reason to trust.
- Also FAIL: the page compares or recommends products the site sells and does not disclose it (see
  Evidence overlays, comparison pages).

**Q14. Recognized authority.** If someone researched the site producing the content, would they
see it as well-trusted or widely recognized as an authority on its topic?
- PASS: the site has, or clearly demonstrates, recognized authority on the topic.
- FAIL: no evident authority or recognition.
- Site-level: N/A if no site context.

**Q15. Expert author.** Is this content written or reviewed by an expert or enthusiast who
demonstrably knows the topic well?
- PASS: demonstrable subject-matter expertise or genuine first-hand enthusiasm is evident.
  An institutional voice ("we tested", "in our warehouse") counts **only when the testing or handling
  actually happened**; invented bylines, invented tests and invented experience do not.
- FAIL: no evidence the author knows the topic well, or expertise is claimed through experience the
  evidence does not support (see Evidence overlays).

**Q16. No factual errors.** Does the content avoid easily verified factual errors?
- PASS: no easily checkable factual errors found.
- FAIL: one or more easily verified factual errors. Count as errors, when checkable: a claim wider than
  its source (one model's spec stated for the whole brand, one retailer's price stated as "the price"),
  an unproven absence stated as fact ("the manufacturer does not publish…"), and a universal or
  superlative word (`all`, `only`, `best`, `never`) the evidence does not cover.
- Note: Google phrases this as "Does the content HAVE any easily-verified factual errors?" PASS
  here means NO errors.

## Section 3: People-First Content (5 questions)

**Q17. Intended audience.** Is there an existing or intended audience for the business/site that
would find the content useful if they came directly to you?
- PASS: clearly serves a real, intended audience beyond search traffic.
- FAIL: no evident audience other than search engines.
- Site-level: N/A if no site context.

**Q18. First-hand expertise / depth.** Does the content clearly demonstrate first-hand expertise
and a depth of knowledge (from actually using a product or service, or visiting a place)?
- PASS: hands-on experience or deep knowledge shown. Deep, well-scoped documentary knowledge (specs,
  manuals, standards applied to the reader's situation) can pass on depth when no hands-on claim is made.
- FAIL: no sign of first-hand experience or depth, **or** first-hand experience is claimed that the
  evidence does not support. A first-person narrator is legitimate only for supplied, real work.

**Q19. Primary purpose.** Does the site have a primary purpose or focus?
- PASS: coherent focus the content fits within.
- FAIL: site appears unfocused or topically scattered.
- Site-level: N/A if no site context.

**Q20. Reader achieves their goal.** After reading, will someone leave feeling they've learned
enough about the topic to help achieve their goal?
- PASS: the reader's goal is satisfied.
- FAIL: reader left with unmet needs or open questions.

**Q21. Satisfying experience.** Will someone reading the content leave feeling they've had a
satisfying experience?
- PASS: the overall experience is satisfying and complete.
- FAIL: unsatisfying or frustrating.

## Section 4: Avoid Search-Engine-First Content (11 questions), reverse-scored

> These are warning signs. **PASS = behavior ABSENT. FAIL = behavior PRESENT.**

**Q22. Not made primarily for search engines.**
- PASS: made for people.
- FAIL: made primarily to attract search-engine visits.

**Q23. Not scattershot topic production.**
- PASS: topics are deliberate and within the site's focus.
- FAIL: lots of content on many topics in hopes some performs.
- Site-level: N/A if no site context.

**Q24. No extensive automation for scale.**
- PASS: no extensive automation used to mass-produce content across topics.
- FAIL: extensive automation/AI churning content across many topics.
- Site-level: N/A if no site context. Note: AI-assisted drafting of one carefully edited article
  is not "extensive automation for scale"; a hundred unedited pages is.

**Q25. Adds value beyond summarizing others.**
- PASS: meaningful value beyond summarizing what others say.
- FAIL: mainly summarizes others without adding much.

**Q26. Not chasing trends out of character.**
- PASS: topics the site would cover for its audience anyway.
- FAIL: written only because it is trending.

**Q27. Reader doesn't need to search again.**
- PASS: reader satisfied, need not search elsewhere.
- FAIL: reader must search again for better information.

**Q28. No arbitrary word-count padding.**
- PASS: length driven by usefulness.
- FAIL: written to a word count because of a belief about a "preferred" length.

**Q29. Real expertise in the niche.**
- PASS: entered the topic area with genuine expertise or interest.
- FAIL: entered mainly to chase search traffic, without real expertise.

**Q30. No false promises of answers.**
- PASS: does not promise answers to questions that have none.
- FAIL: promises answers that don't exist (unconfirmed dates, etc.), or the title promises a definitive
  winner the body's evidence cannot deliver.

**Q31. No fake freshness via date changes.**
- PASS: dates reflect real, substantial updates. Publication, update, source-retrieval and test dates
  are kept distinct, and an "updated" date describes real work.
- FAIL: page dates changed to look fresh without substantive changes, or a new year in the title while
  the evidence (prices, picks, specs) was not re-checked.
- Site-level / history: N/A for a first-publication draft.

**Q32. No churn for the sake of "freshness".**
- PASS: content not added or removed merely to seem fresh.
- FAIL: large content changes mainly to appear fresh. Age alone, or a traffic dip alone, is not a reason
  to rewrite sound passages.
- Site-level / history: N/A for a first-publication draft.

## Evidence overlays (inform existing verdicts; not extra questions)

These checks never add points or questions. They supply evidence for verdicts on the 32 questions,
using the same evidence rules as `information-gain/references/evidence-ledger.md`. This file is not included in this repository.

**Ledger-aware grading.** If the draft came from `/seo-content` and a research dossier or run
`research.md` exists, read its evidence ledger. A material claim with no ledger record, or worded wider
than its record, is evidence against Q16 (and Q13 if the page claims to be sourced). Do not run new
research to rescue a claim; the grader may open a cited source to check an easily verified fact.

**Invented experience.** "We tested", "in our experience", "after installing dozens", named reviewers or
customers, and first-person anecdotes need a real record (supplied test notes, the store's own data, a
named contributor). With no record: FAIL Q15 and Q18 for that claim, and note it as a top fix. Never
suggest inventing experience as the fix; suggest documentary depth or getting real input.

**Comparison pages (X vs Y, alternatives, best-X).** Check, and map failures to these questions:

| Check | Evidence against |
|---|---|
| A winner, edge or "better" claim rests on evidence for only one side | Q16, Q20 |
| Criteria compared on different bases (a measured spec vs a marketing adjective) | Q16 |
| Cheaper option wins on price but lacks the capability being compared | Q16, Q20 |
| A product the site sells is not disclosed, is judged on different criteria, or its CTA comes before the verdict | Q13, Q22 |
| Missing data written as "not publicly available" / "does not publish" without proof | Q16 |
| Prices or specs undated | Q16, Q31 |
| Competitors caricatured, genuine strengths omitted | Q13, Q21 |
| Tie, stay or neither was the honest answer but a universal winner is declared | Q20, Q30 |

**Opening swap test.** Could the opening fit an unrelated article after swapping the topic or product
name? If yes, that is evidence against Q3 (nothing beyond the obvious) and Q7.

---

## Optional: authorship transparency (not scored)

If automation or AI generation was used substantially, the content ideally discloses it, explains
how it was used, and why. Mention this in the report as guidance; do not include it in the
32-point score unless the user asks to weight it.

---

## Output the grader produces

1. A per-section table: each question, PASS / FAIL / PARTIAL / N/A, one-line justification
   quoting or referencing the content.
2. Section subtotals (points earned / weighted possible).
3. Overall Score as a percentage and letter grade, mode stated.
4. Top 3-5 highest-impact fixes, ordered by how much they raise the score.
5. One-paragraph plain-English verdict and the single most important next step.
