# House spelling and punctuation

Applies to every word a reader or listener meets under the company's name, and to anything a skill emits that a person will read.

## Spelling: British

Every word a reader sees under the company's name uses British spelling: centre, fibre, metre (as a length), colour, behaviour, catalogue, defence, licence (the noun), programme (not software), grey, aluminium, labelled, modelling, fulfil. This covers titles, descriptions, captions, on-screen and burned-in text, thumbnails, docs pages, articles, channel text and replies.

Use "-ise", not "-ize" (organise, optimise, utilisation, analyse). Assumption: the instruction did not say which British form is meant; "-ise" follows `<house locale>` usage.

### Never respelled

Respelling would break accuracy or a link in these cases:

- Proper nouns and third-party names ("YouTube Partner Program", "[product]")
- Quoted titles of sources and of our own published articles
- URLs, slugs and filenames (/blog/example-slug/ stays)
- Code, CSS, API fields and protocol names (Authorization, color)
- Verbatim console and product labels ([console label])
- Words where the US form is also correct British in that sense: a billing meter, a software program, "license" as a verb

Examples held as written: YouTube Partner Program, Pixabay Content License, Standard YouTube License, Creative Commons License, [console label], Authorization: Bearer, Authorization header.

### What the audience reads, and what only the platform reads

British spelling applies to what a person reads: titles, descriptions, captions, on-screen and thumbnail text, page body and alt text. Fields only the platform reads follow the platform's usual form, or whatever performs best there: tags, channel keywords, upload filenames, slugs and hashtags. Those use the form people search with, for example the US form of a [topic] term and its hashtag. Assumption, unmeasured: the US form is the more searched one.

### Text fed to a voice engine

Text fed to a voice engine whose pronunciation dictionary cannot read a British form may keep US spellings. The script a person reads, and every caption and on-screen line, stay British.

### Never import spelling from a source

A US form in a source is not evidence of house style. Convert spelling as the copy is written.

### Word list

US form to British form: center to centre; centers to centres; centered to centred; centering to centring; fiber to fibre; fibers to fibres; kilometer to kilometre; kilometers to kilometres; centimeter to centimetre; centimeters to centimetres; millimeter to millimetre; millimeters to millimetres; nanometer to nanometre; nanometers to nanometres; color to colour; colors to colours; colored to coloured; coloring to colouring; colorful to colourful; behavior to behaviour; behaviors to behaviours; behavioral to behavioural; favorite to favourite; favorites to favourites; favor to favour; favorable to favourable; honor to honour; labor to labour; neighbor to neighbour; neighbors to neighbours; flavor to flavour; humor to humour; rumor to rumour; harbor to harbour; vapor to vapour; endeavor to endeavour; savior to saviour; defense to defence; offense to offence; catalog to catalogue; catalogs to catalogues; gray to grey; aluminum to aluminium; fulfill to fulfil; fulfills to fulfils; fulfillment to fulfilment; enroll to enrol; enrolls to enrols; enrollment to enrolment; skillful to skilful; labeled to labelled; labeling to labelling; modeled to modelled; modeling to modelling; traveled to travelled; traveling to travelling; canceled to cancelled; canceling to cancelling; signaled to signalled; signaling to signalling; fueled to fuelled; fueling to fuelling; leveled to levelled; leveling to levelling; totaled to totalled; totaling to totalling; channeled to channelled; channeling to channelling; tunneled to tunnelled; tunneling to tunnelling; counselor to counsellor; jewelry to jewellery.

Spelling that depends on the sense:

- meter: metre, when it means a length (a billing or power meter stays 'meter')
- meters: metres, when it means a length (billing or power meters stay 'meters')
- license: licence as a noun; 'license' is correct British as a verb
- licenses: licences as a noun; 'licenses' is correct British as a verb
- program: programme, except software and computer programs, which stay 'program'
- programs: programmes, except software and computer programs
- analog: analogue
- dialog: dialogue, except a UI dialog box
- artifact: artefact is the British form; check the surface before changing

Suffix rules: -ize, -ization and related forms become -ise, -isation (organise, utilisation); -yze forms become -yse (analyse, catalyse). Words where "iz" is not the suffix stay as they are: size, sizes, sized, sizing, resize, resizes, resized, resizing, downsize, downsized, downsizing, oversize, oversized, upsize, prize, prizes, seize, seizes, seized, seizing, capsize, capsized, maize, baize, assize, citizen, citizens, wizard, wizards, bizarre, horizon, horizons, horizontal, horizontally, denizen, frizz, blizzard, lizard, ozone, prizewinning.

The list is not exhaustive. A US form missing from it is still wrong; add the pair to the list when a miss turns up.

## Punctuation: no em dash

No em dash in anything sent to another person. Use commas or colons, or restructure the sentence. This applies to every message, brief, task description and document, and to any external content.

The rule covers every surface a person will read, not only body text: titles, file and folder names, share notes, frontmatter, captions, alt text, commit messages and JSON values. A strip applied to body text alone is not enough. Strip at the boundary where text is emitted, and never write an em dash by hand into a title or a folder name.

Contractions are fine in narration and on-screen text.
