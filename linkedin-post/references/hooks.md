# Hook formulas

Twenty-one hook formulas for line 1 of a LinkedIn feed post. Each has a template, a filled example,
what it is for, and the trap that usually ruins it.

Adapted from the li-post hooks list of `linkedin-agent-skill` by Jake Schincariol
(https://github.com/Jakeschincariol/linkedin-agent-skill), MIT licence, Copyright (c) 2026 Jake
Schincariol. Pack changes: British spelling, a second-person note on the formulas that address the
reader, and the hook rules from the videos (see the `linkedin-strategy` playbook reference, section 8).

## Why line 1 matters

The feed truncates at roughly 210 characters on desktop and 140 on mobile before "see more", so the
hook has to survive alone. Readers see the first two or three lines and the media. Every line has to
earn the next one.

## Rules

1. One idea per hook. If it needs a comma splice, it needs two posts.
2. Line 2 is not a hook. It is the payoff of line 1. Never waste it on setup.
3. The line under the hook is the re-hook: it answers the reader's first objection ("even if...",
   "without...", often in brackets). The hook makes them stop. The re-hook makes them stay.
4. No question mark in line 1 unless the question is so specific that only this poster could ask it.
5. Numbers beat adjectives. "$4,200" beats "a lot of money".
6. If the hook works with any other person's name on it, it is not this account's hook.
7. Line 1 follows the first-line limit in the layout record (a prior from the videos: short is better,
   and starting with a number or "how" helps, 058).
8. Three curiosity-gap mechanisms work well: two contradictory things paired, a decision revealed
   without the reason, or a start at the moment of change (017).
9. Never invent the number or the story. A missing figure is a bracketed placeholder such as `[figure to confirm]`.

**Second person.** The voice record for the Page bars "you", "your" and "yours" in every post.
Rewrite the formulas marked [2P] in the third person, with the reader named by noun. A worked
variant is given for each.

## The 21

| # | Name | Template | Example | Best for | Trap |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Contrarian Take | Everyone says {common advice}. After {specific experience}, I think that is wrong. | Everyone says post daily. I posted 4x a week for a year and beat every daily poster in my niche. | A point of view. The highest comment rate of the 21 (creator's claim). | Being contrarian about something nobody believes. The belief must be genuinely common. |
| 2 | Number Reveal | I {did thing} for {N} {days/weeks}. Here is what actually happened. | I sent 400 cold DMs in 30 days. 11 replied. 2 became clients. | Experiments, challenges, anything with a countable result. | Burying the number. Put it in line 1. |
| 3 | Mistake Confession | {Specific cost} is what {one mistake} cost me. | $18,000 is what not having a contract cost me last March. | Trust. People forward the posts where the poster looks bad. | A fake-humble mistake. "I care too much" reads as a brag. |
| 4 | Before / After | {Time ago} I was {low state}. Today I {high state}. The difference was {one thing}. | 18 months ago I was writing proposals at 2am. Today I send them in 6 minutes. One template did it. | Proof the poster has travelled the distance the reader wants to travel. | Three-part transformations. Name one lever. |
| 5 | The List Promise | {N} things I wish someone had told me before {milestone}. | 7 things I wish someone had told me before I hired my first employee. | Saves. The highest save rate of the 21 (creator's claim). | N above 10. Nobody believes 15 good ones. |
| 6 | Insider Secret | After {N} years {doing thing}, here is the part nobody puts in the {job posting/course/pitch}. | After 9 years in agency sales, here is the part nobody puts in the job posting. | Credentialed takes. Only if the years are real. | Promising a secret and delivering common knowledge. |
| 7 | The Callout [2P] | If you are still {doing outdated thing}, stop. | If you are still writing your LinkedIn posts in the LinkedIn composer, stop. | Short posts with one instruction. The fastest to write. | Calling out something nobody does, or the audience with contempt. |
| 8 | Question Trap [2P] | {Specific scenario with a real cost}. What do you do? | Your best client asks for a 40% discount and hints they will leave without it. What do you do? | Comment volume. People answer scenarios, not "thoughts?". | A question with an obvious right answer. There must be a real fork. |
| 9 | Story Cold Open | "{Line of dialogue}" then {who said it, when, and why it mattered}. | "We are going with someone cheaper." That email arrived 40 minutes after I sent my best proposal of the year. | Long storytelling posts. The highest dwell time. | Dialogue nobody would say. Use the real line, ugly parts included. |
| 10 | The Receipt | {Screenshot or hard number}. {One sentence of context}. | 3 clients, 11 days, zero calls booked. Here is the exact sequence that did it. | Anything provable. Pair with an image (reach roughly doubles, creator's claim). | A receipt with no story. The number opens the door; the story is the room. |
| 11 | Myth Bust | {Popular explanation} is not why {bad outcome} is happening. | The algorithm is not why posts are dying. | Reframing. Sets up the real cause in line 3. | Busting the myth and never naming the replacement. |
| 12 | The Comparison | {Expensive option} vs {cheap option}. {Surprising verdict}. | A $12,000 brand agency vs a $20 tool and a weekend. The weekend won. | Tool and process posts. A very high share rate. | An unfair comparison. Concede what the expensive option does better. |
| 13 | Permission Slip [2P] | You are allowed to {thing the reader feels guilty about}. | You are allowed to turn down work that pays well and makes you miserable. | Reach beyond the niche. | Generic self-care. It must fit the reader's working life. |
| 14 | Pattern Interrupt | {One word or a three-word line, full stop.} | I got fired. | Stopping the scroll cold. | Overuse. Once a month at most. |
| 15 | The Warning [2P] | {Common practice} is quietly costing you {specific thing}. | Your "quick call to align" is quietly costing you six hours a week. | Problem-aware readers who have not linked cause to cost. | Fear with no exit. Give the fix in the same post. |
| 16 | Good vs Great | Good {role} {do X}. Great {role} {do Y}. | Good salespeople answer the question. Great salespeople ask why it was asked. | Quotable one-liners. | A distinction that only means "try harder". Y must be a different action. |
| 17 | Time Anchor | {Task} used to take me {long time}. It now takes {short time}. | Writing a week of posts used to take me 5 hours. It now takes 20 minutes. | Tool, system and automation posts. The strongest how-to shape. | An unbelievable ratio. 5 hours to 20 minutes lands; 5 hours to 8 seconds does not. |
| 18 | The Unpopular Rule | I do not {widely accepted practice}. Ever. Here is why. | I do not take discovery calls. Ever. | Positioning. Attracts the right clients by repelling the wrong ones. | A rule the poster breaks constantly. |
| 19 | Curiosity Gap | The {superlative} {thing} I ever {verb} {broke the obvious rule}. | The best hire I ever made failed the interview. | Openers that force the "see more" tap. | A gap never closed. Pay it off by line 4. |
| 20 | The Walk-Away | I {fired/quit/deleted/cancelled} {valuable thing}. {Result}. | I fired my highest-paying client in January. Revenue is up 30%. | Values posts with a business result. | Walking away at no cost. Name what it cost. |
| 21 | The Direct Value | Here is the exact {artefact} I used to {specific outcome}. Steal it. | Here is the exact 4-line email I used to reopen 3 dead deals. Steal it. | Lead magnets. The highest conversion of the 21 (creator's claim). | Making people ask for something that could have been posted. Give it away in the post. This pack never uses "comment X to get it". |

The repo examples are illustrations, not facts about any account. Never reuse an example number.

## Third-person variants for the [2P] formulas

| # | Third-person variant |
|---|---|
| 7 | Most {role}s still {outdated thing}. It is time to stop. |
| 8 | {Specific scenario with a real cost}. Most {role}s pick the wrong option. |
| 13 | {Role}s are allowed to {thing they feel guilty about}. |
| 15 | {Common practice} quietly costs {role}s {specific thing}. |

The weekday templates from 047 and 103 ("Your [problem] is costing you [thing]") take the same
change: "[Problem] is costing [role]s [thing]".

## Matching hooks to post types

A working map for this pack, built from each formula's "best for" line; the sources give no such table.

| Post type | Formulas that usually fit |
|---|---|
| Story (origin, mistake, day in the life) | 3, 9, 14, 19, 20 |
| Opinion (contrarian, mythbuster, hot take) | 1, 11, 16, 18 |
| Teach (framework, workflow, pro tips, checklist) | 5, 17, 21, 12 |
| Proof (customer result, behind the numbers) | 2, 4, 10 |
| Trend or news take | 11, 1, 15 |
| Objection or FAQ | 8, 11, 15 |
| Offer | 21, 18 |

## Hook effort

- Write at least five hook variants from different formulas before choosing (079 tip 8). Show three,
  and name the one to ship and why in one sentence.
- Spend about half the drafting time on the first two lines (099) and most of the rest on packaging
  (079 tip 7).
- Lead with the best result and use a specific number where the facts ledger allows one (079 tips 9
  and 12).
