## How to read this file

- **A cite** is `[id @handle date]` for a video, for example `[021 @mollystovold 2026-09-02]`, and
  `[repo li-post]` for a file in the repo. The permalink for every video is in the source index.
- **Creators' claims.** Every revenue, follower, impression, client and study figure is the
  creator's own claim. None was verified. Never repeat one as fact in a post. They are kept here
  because they show what each creator is selling and how much weight a rule deserves.
- **Audio only** marks a detail that was spoken but not written in the caption. Whisper
  transcripts mishear names; known mishearings are recorded where they matter.
- **Pack decisions win.** Where a source disagrees with a decision made for this pack (links,
  hashtags, contractions, cadence, length, posting time, weekends, bait, spelling), the decision is
  stated next to the source rule. The source is recorded as written, not obeyed.
- **Rejected items** are kept so the skills know what not to do. See "Rejected and out of scope".

## 2. The 2026 algorithm

### What the feed rewards

- **Expertise-weighted ranking.** LinkedIn's new algorithm, rolled out early 2026, is an
  "expertise-weighted" model that constantly adapts to user actions. It replaces the old
  engagement-based system. Hashtags and likes no longer drive distribution. The algorithm uses AI
  to read the semantic meaning of content to gauge expertise, then checks how others interact,
  specifically saves, sends and comments. [021 @mollystovold 2026-09-02]
- **Saves and long comments.** Citing a study of 621,000 posts: saves drive 5x more reach than a
  like; longer comments are weighted 15x more (creator's claim; the study is not named).
  [021 @mollystovold 2026-09-02]
- **A long comment counts as four likes; saves and shares are the most powerful signals.** The
  transcript renders "shares" as "sends": "saves and sends are still the most powerful thing".
  [045 @mollystovold 2026-10-14]
- **Dwell time.** "The only thing LinkedIn measures is whether people stopped scrolling and how
  long they stayed." [099 @aleckremins 2026-10-04]
- **Order of reward.** "LinkedIn rewards dwell time, saves and comments, in that order." [repo
  li-post]
- **Keywords everywhere.** Use the same keywords in posts as in the profile, the same language
  everywhere, "even in your comments" (audio only). [021 @mollystovold 2026-09-02] Naturally
  include the keywords to be known for (for example LinkedIn marketing, personal branding, content
  creation) in comments, because the algorithm is expertise-weighted, but never at the expense of
  sounding natural: "obviously, we're not going to do this at the expense of sounding natural...
  that is the priority" (audio only). [042 @mollystovold 2026-10-13]
- **Write for saves and sends.** Reframe beliefs to encourage saves and shares instead of
  validating opinions. Create content that sparks debate in the comments: longer comments come from
  people really wanting to understand something or dispute something (audio only).
  [021 @mollystovold 2026-09-02]
- **Author's context claims:** 98% of LinkedIn users have experienced a massive decrease in
  impressions; she built a seven-figure personal brand on LinkedIn; a part two on creating content
  for the new algorithm was promised. [021 @mollystovold 2026-09-02]

### The first 90 minutes and the first hour

- **The golden hour is dead.** "The golden hour of LinkedIn is a thing of the past." The old
  tactic was to post at 8am and quickly comment "thanks LinkedIn for another viral post"; feeds
  were flooded with meaningless one-line comments, so it no longer works. Audio only: the ritual
  was "bish, bash, bosh. Thanks LinkedIn for another viral post"; the 8am comments were
  "congratulations on your new role, great post... shitty one-liners that literally gave us
  nothing". [045 @mollystovold 2026-10-14]
- **The first 90 minutes of interactions dictate reach.** [045 @mollystovold 2026-10-14] The owned
  audience list is people who consistently engage back within the first 90 minutes of posting.
  [042 @mollystovold 2026-10-13]
- **First hour.** Reply to every comment on the post within the first hour, especially the first
  15 minutes. [101 @aleckremins 2026-11-04] "Do everything you need to do in the first hour after
  you post" (the next video in the series covers distribution). [098 @aleckremins 2026-11-02]
- **Reply thread.** "The reply thread under your own post is where reach is actually decided.
  Every reply is a second engagement event on the post, and the first hour of replies does most of
  the work." [repo li-reply]

### When to post

- **Post when the ideal clients are online** and able to leave thoughtful comments, and when the
  poster can be present to engage. Molly moved from 8am UK time to 1pm UK time (aligned with the US
  morning); screenshots show meaningful comments after the switch; reach and lead quality both
  increased. Audio only: the US audience leaves "really thoughtful, insightful comments"; she was
  missing out by posting while they were asleep; the tweak made the biggest difference to her reach,
  growth speed and the quality of leads into her agency. Moral: test when the audience is active
  and make sure someone can interact, or outsource engagement ("make sure to outsource that to an
  agency like mine", audio only). [045 @mollystovold 2026-10-14]
- **Repo default.** Post when the audience is at a desk. For a B2B audience in one time zone,
  Tuesday to Thursday, 7:30 to 9:30am local, is the working default, with Monday afternoon and
  Friday morning as the second tier. Weekends are for personal-story posts or nothing. "The day and
  hour matter far less than whether the first line is good." Anchor times to the audience's time
  zone, not the poster's. [repo li-plan]
- **Pack decision:** posting time is set per account in its strategy page: the
  audience zone plus a person on duty for the first 90 minutes. Weekend slots are allowed only when
  the account strategy calls for them.

### Links, hashtags and length

- **Links.** Repo: "No links in the post body. LinkedIn suppresses posts with outbound links. Put
  the link in the first comment and say so in the receipt." [repo li-post] Molly: put a link in the
  bottom-of-funnel post once a week despite the debate about links: "You need to be making money on
  LinkedIn" (audio only). [068 @mollystovold 2026-09-14] **Pack decision:** a link appears only in a
  convert post. Most posts carry no link. Never use the first-comment trick; this pack cannot post
  comments and `social-content` bans moving a link to the comments.
- **Hashtags.** Hashtags no longer drive distribution. [021 @mollystovold 2026-09-02] "Stop. Using.
  Hashtags." is Molly's example of a contrarian take. [080 @mollystovold 2026-10-29] Repo: three
  hashtags maximum, at the bottom, only if they are real categories someone follows; the hashtag
  wall (5+) is a structural tell. [repo li-post, li-human] **Pack decision:** no hashtags.
- **Length.** Repo: 900 to 1,300 characters is the working range for a text post; under 400 reads
  as a thought; over 2,000 must earn every line, and line 2 must pay for the "see more" tap.
  [repo li-post] Di Bratto: write only as long as needed; "make every line fight for its life"
  (audio only). [017 @dibratto 2026-09-02] **Pack decision:** feed posts 500 to 1,300 characters;
  LinkedIn articles are a separate shape with no fixed length.
- **Truncation.** The feed truncates at roughly 210 characters on desktop and 140 on mobile before
  "see more", so the hook has to survive alone. [repo hooks.json] People see the first two to three
  lines. [058 @laraacostar 2026-10-31] The reader sees the first line and a half of text, then the
  media (audio only). [040 @dibratto 2026-10-07]

### Platform behaviour and norms

- **LinkedIn in search and AI answers.** LinkedIn is cited in Google AI Overviews; "LinkedIn is one
  of the top sources for Gemini and Google AI overviews" (audio only). [085 @avigrondin 2026-11-01]
  The most-clicked websites in search are user-generated content platforms: YouTube, Reddit,
  TikTok, LinkedIn, Instagram, Facebook, X (chart: most-clicked domains after search interaction,
  Q2 2026). [086 @edward.builds 2026-10-28]
- **LinkedIn is slow.** Trends appear on X days earlier than on LinkedIn: "LinkedIn is always so
  late. Everything moves so, so slowly there and by the time something hits LinkedIn, it's already
  been chewed up on X for days" (audio only). [053 @diandraescobar 2026-10-01]
- **Least saturated.** LinkedIn is often seen as cringe and a job board, but is the least saturated
  major platform. [065 @laraacostar 2026-09-25]
- **Corporate norms.** "LinkedIn isn't cringe, corporate is, and LinkedIn is a corporate platform.
  And so therefore, LinkedIn is cringe as a result." Users try to appease a corporate tone rather
  than being themselves. Even the rudest LinkedIn user will only say "that's rubbish", unlike other
  platforms ("on Instagram, there'd be like a bot account, no profile, and they'll be coming after
  you"). "Are people real on Instagram? Are people real on TikTok? No, people are just tired of work
  and work politics, but no social media platform is real." Swearing, even as a joke, gets a post
  deleted: "You can't swear much on LinkedIn... If you say [an expletive], like just joking, they
  delete your post. But they don't know that you're joking. That's the problem. Who decides? The
  LinkedIn gods." (Whisper rendered the last line "Who's nay? The LinkedIn gods".)
  r/LinkedInLunatics (https://www.reddit.com/r/LinkedInLunatics/) is where people screenshot
  LinkedIn posts to mock them, "which is honestly a bit scary". The video is a conversation (an
  interviewer asks "what's the problem with LinkedIn?"); there is no caption and no how-to.
  [063 @laraacostar 2026-10-17] **For this pack:** no swearing; write so a post would not end up mocked
  out of context.
- **Commenting lifts profile views.** Per LinkedIn's team (as reported by the creator), commenting
  once a week can triple profile views. [101 @aleckremins 2026-11-04]
- **Introduction posts.** "LinkedIn loves an introduction post" (audio only). [048 @laraacostar
  2026-10-20]
- **Pods are visible.** Commenters accused Lara of relying on engagement pods rather than content
  quality. [034 @laraacostar 2026-08-10] An earlier note said AI
  detects engagement pods via comment velocity and patterns; that note had no source.

## 3. Strategy frameworks

### The 6-step founder-led strategy [083 @dibratto 2026-10-11]

Creator has built strategies for 50+ founders backed by YC, a16z and more.

1. **Define the ICP:** job title, industry, company size, responsibilities. Audio: "figure out
   exactly who you're trying to reach in terms of job title, industry, headcount, etc."
2. **Define the objective:** pipeline, credibility with buyers, investors, brand awareness, or
   recruiting talent. Audio: "What do you want LinkedIn to accomplish for your business? Is it brand
   awareness? Is it generating pipeline? Is it recruiting better talent? You define it."
3. **Define positioning:** the three things someone should associate with the poster after 10
   posts. Audio: "What do you want these people to know and like and trust you for? ... the three
   things you want someone to associate you with after reading 10 or so of your posts".
4. **Build a content funnel:** top (topics the ICP already cares about, "engineered to get you that
   visibility you want"), middle (frameworks, processes and opinions that demonstrate expertise),
   bottom (case studies, customer results, testimonials, FAQs and objections).
5. **Generate post ideas** from industry conversations, customer questions, sales calls ("sales
   calls, those are a gold mine") and first-hand experience. For each idea "decide the takeaway and
   figure out how to package it to be as compelling as possible for LinkedIn ahead of writing it".
6. **Set up an execution system:** commit to 3 to 5 posts a week, build a backlog, capture
   experiences ("set time aside to capture your expertise and or have someone on your team help you
   to do so"), review every 30 days to see which topics attract actual buyers ("specifically, pay
   attention to what content actually attracts the right buyers").

End card (Whisper garble): "If you want to surround this for you, book a call for my bio",
presumably an offer to run the strategy via a call link in bio.

### The 5-stage founder strategy [022 @dibratto 2026-09-06]

The agency's process for building presences for 50+ founders, shown live on Cognition (the company
behind Devin); the founder is named as Scott (audio only). It starts from what the buyer already
cares about and works backwards (audio only).

1. **Research:** what the founder sells, who they sell to (CTOs, VP Engineering, Heads of Eng), and
   what those buyers care about: content they consume, day-to-day questions, current versus desired
   state, pain points.
2. **Content funnel (3 parts):** top of funnel for visibility (founder stories, hiring, AI takes);
   middle for trust (engineering opinions, frameworks, news commentary); bottom for conversion (case
   studies such as Mercedes-Benz, FedRAMP and cost savings; audio adds Devin use cases, customer
   FAQs and objection handling).
3. **Distribution:** posting alone is not enough; send 100 connection requests per week to ICPs;
   once they accept, the founder's content shows in their feed (audio only). **Rejected for this pack**
   (section 17).
4. **Tracking:** track which ICP accounts view, like or comment, and pass warm leads to sales:
   weekly tracking of ICP profile viewers, likers and engagers, sent to the sales team for warm
   outbound (audio only).
5. **Iterate weekly:** track impressions, profile visits, and best-performing topics, formats,
   hooks, length and media type; "do a lot more of what works and a lot less of what doesn't"
   (audio only).

### The four-part content strategy and the 40/40/20 mix [066 @aleckremins 2026-10-25]

Part 3 of the "0 to 10k followers LinkedIn Speedrun Growth Series": "Create the most dangerous
LinkedIn content Strategy". The difference between zero traction and millions of impressions is a
simple content strategy. Credibility claim: the agency works with companies backed by Lightspeed,
A16Z, Founders Fund and Y Combinator. Full breakdown offered via comment "STRATEGY" (the strategy
card).

- **Framing on screen:** 1) Cadence, 2) The Pillars, 3) Actual Content, 4) Breakdowns. The audio's
  order is pillars, goal per post, 40/40/20 mix, then cadence.
- **Pillars:** pick 3 to 5 based on what the ICP struggles with, what the poster is a world expert
  in, what the poster is passionate about, and what ties back to the ICP's pain points. Audio: 85%
  of B2B decision makers say someone understanding their problem is a huge factor in who they buy
  from (creator's stat).
- **Every post serves one goal:** Reach (TOFU), Trust (MOFU) or Convert (BOFU), split 40/40/20.
  Most people starting off are not ready to buy, so BOFU is the smallest share (audio only).
- **Cadence:** 3 to 5 posts a week. Audio: Buffer found that the more times someone posts, the more
  successful each post is likely to be (creator's claim about Buffer). Stick with the cadence for
  at least a month before upping it or trying something new.
- Turn pillars into specific ideas (see POST, section 4).

### 4-3-2-1 [034 @laraacostar 2026-08-10]

Four posts a week, three content pillars, two audiences, one lead magnet, "so he'd never sit down
not knowing what to write again". Steps: figure out positioning; build three pillars; pull the two

## 4. Writing frameworks

### THBM: Topic, Hook, Body, Media [017 @dibratto 2026-09-02]

Creator's claim: 5,000+ posts written for 50+ founders backed by Y Combinator, Accel and a16z, with
screenshot proof of high impressions. Free 23-page LinkedIn mini-course ("100% free") offered via
comment "LINKEDIN".

- **Topic:** convey one clear idea that makes readers know, like or trust the poster: stories,
  frameworks or opinions. Cut anything that does not support the point.
- **Hook:** create a "curiosity gap" so readers click "see more". Three mechanisms: (1) pairing
  contradictory things, (2) revealing a decision without the reason, (3) starting at a moment of
  change. Examples (audio only): "I run a 100-person marketing agency, but I've never had a
  marketing job"; "Last month, I turned down the biggest client our agency had ever been offered";
  "Ten minutes into my first board meeting, the CEO closes [his] laptop and asks everyone to leave".
- **Body**, three rules: write only as long as needed ("make every line fight for its life", audio
  only); use line breaks to separate ideas and arguments, not individual lines (corrected from the
  audio); follow a logical order, Hook > Context > Body > CTA, written for a cold reader who does
  not know who the poster is or what they do.
- **Media:** match visuals to post type: founder story = photo; deep listicle = carousel; dense
  process = infographic; industry commentary = news headline or tweet screenshot. Readers scrolling
  the feed see only the hook and the media, so optimise the media for that first impression (audio
  only).

### CPIO: Convey, Packaging, Information, Order [018 @dibratto 2026-08-29]

The team's "secret weapon" for posts for venture-backed founders, developed after personally
writing 5,000 of them (creator's claim; screenshots of posts with hundreds of thousands of
impressions).

- **C, Convey:** define what is being communicated (story, opinion, process, case study).
- **P, Packaging:** present it in the most compelling format, for example a carousel for lists, a
  tweet screenshot for industry commentary, a cheat sheet for dense processes. "Arguably the most
  important" step (audio only); it is a muscle built over time.
- **I, Information:** decide what must be included to make the point, and what does NOT need to be
  in the post (audio only).
- **O, Order:** arrange information so it makes sense to a third-party reader. Test with
  ChatGPT or Claude: ask it to act as a cold reader and see what it thinks (audio only).

## 6. Formats

### The timeline (introduction) format [048 @laraacostar 2026-10-20]

A timeline story listing year-by-year wins and fails (example: got kicked out of school, nearly went
broke, then built a million-user platform), formatted to be skimmable, not a long novel. "People
love to see the journey, but only when it's formatted just like that. It makes it skimmable rather
than another LinkedIn novel that people don't know the point of." (audio) Commenters debated it
("Nobody's reading that long post on LinkedIn").

### The starting-line format [048 @laraacostar 2026-10-20]

State exactly where the poster starts (for example 300 followers, zero engagement), promise to post
every week mapping the journey, and say who they are, the goal, and what each week will cover, so
people anticipate following. Audio: "300 followers, zero engagement and no connections... map out
exactly what you wanna post on LinkedIn, who you are, what your goal is, exactly what you're gonna
be talking about every single week. So people sort of anticipate what they are going to be following
you for." Her first viral post: half a million impressions in 42 hours (creator's claim); "it still
works incredibly well till this day because LinkedIn loves an introduction post". Audio opener:
"trick the LinkedIn algorithm". The closing line "For this, you just need to use Clio. You give it
your story and it takes care of everything else using those two formats" is unclear: "Clio" is not
established as a tool and is not recommended. Masterclass via comment "event".

### The simulated conversation [094 @dibratto 2026-11-04]

A realistic back-and-forth between someone in a job role and a more senior decision-maker (for
example CEO and VP of Marketing). The senior person states a common misconception or objection; the
junior person challenges it, explains why it is wrong or incomplete, and teaches what to do instead.
The author calls it "a simulated conversation" (audio). Used for four years; analytics shown from
199,474 to 417,230 impressions (creator's claims). Author runs social content for 20+ founders
(Founder @ Hat Tip) and pushes this format on every one.

Steps: pick a role and a more senior decision-maker; the senior states the misconception; the junior
challenges it; teach what to do instead. On-screen examples:

1. The CEO wants to cut the content budget to $0 because AI can write posts; the VP argues that is
   why they need more budget.
2. The CEO wants to hire a creator with 200K followers; the Head of Marketing declines because her
   audience may not transfer.
3. The VP of Marketing deletes 340,000 inactive email contacts despite six years of effort.

### Carousels (document posts) [repo li-carousel]

Highest-dwell format because a swipe is counted and a scroll is not. Use when the idea has
sequence (steps, a countdown, a before and after progression, a framework with parts); use a text
post when the idea is one claim.

- 8 to 12 slides. Under 8 is a text post; over 12 and completion falls off.
- Structure: 1 cover (the hook, six words or fewer, plus one line of promise); 2 the stake (why it
  matters, one sentence); 3 to N one idea per slide (a 3 to 7 word headline, at most 25 words
  under it); N+1 recap (the whole thing as a list, so the screenshot is useful); last, the CTA (one
  action: follow, comment a keyword, or the link).
- Slide 1 is 80% of the result. Number every slide (3/10). No slide is a paragraph. The recap slide
  is the one people screenshot. The handle on every slide, small, bottom corner.
- PDF: 1080x1350 (4:5), under 100MB, under 300 pages; HTML with one section per slide, one accent
  colour, type no smaller than 28px; use the brand design system if there is one.
- The post text above the carousel is 2 to 3 lines and is the actual hook in the feed.
- Conflicts: Di Bratto says cheat sheets and carousels are "not landing all that well right now"
  [040]; Di Bratto [017] and the atomizer still pair listicles with carousels. This pack keeps
  carousels in `content-atomizer`.

### Repurposing [repo li-repurpose]

One long asset holds four to six posts. Extract, do not summarise: claims, numbers, stories,
mechanisms, mistakes and quotable lines, with counts, before writing. Fewer than four items means
the asset is thin; say so. Each post stands alone ("Never write 'as I mentioned in my latest
video'"). Vary hook formulas. Order: the strongest claim first, the story midweek, the mechanism
last. Draft one at a time. This pack keeps repurposing in `content-atomizer`.

- **Short answer-first article:** post short LinkedIn articles under 330 words that answer a query

## 7. The 12-point checklist and the 21 tips

### The 12-point checklist [067 @dibratto 2026-10-26]

Whether a post will take off, from ghostwriting 6,000 posts. Each point is shown with an example post
from a different founder or CEO (not named in the notes).

1. **Worth sharing:** makes the reader look good.
2. **Useful today:** something the reader could implement now (spoken: "makes the reader think, I
   could implement this today").
3. **Worth saving:** lasting value, such as a list, process or framework.
4. **Big topic or clear outcome:** broad contentious topics, or a promise of hyper-actionable
   outcomes.
5. **Answers objections:** addresses the obvious objection within the post.
6. **Relevant media:** media that strengthens the hook.
7. **Earns "see more":** a hook that gives the reader no choice but to press see more.
8. **No filler:** only as long as it is valuable.
9. **Works for strangers:** written for a cold audience without prior knowledge.
10. **Simple language:** intentionally very simple language anyone can understand.
11. **Easy to skim:** formatted for skimming.
12. **Sentences connect:** each sentence connects naturally to the next.

### The 21 tips [079 @dibratto 2026-10-19]

"How to write so customers come to you on LinkedIn (From a tech CEO ghostwriter)". Glosses (audio)
in brackets.

1. Write for strangers; assume nobody knows who the poster is.
2. Mine real conversations, objections and personal problems faced in the role.
3. Make readers smarter (topics that make the ICP "feel smarter and want to share with their
   colleagues").
4. Work backwards from what the reader should feel or believe.
5. Add own insight, story, take, opinion, framework or process ("include something proprietary").
6. Package the idea before writing ("a story, a take, a framework, or opinion can all communicate
   the same point very differently").
7. Spend 80% of the time on hook and packaging.
8. Draft five hooks and choose the best ("write at least five hook variations before choosing
   one").
9. Lead with the best result.
10. Start at the most interesting part of the story ("do not bury the lead... start at the most
    interesting part and fill in the gaps afterwards").
11. State the outcome before the steps when teaching a process.
12. Use specific numbers in the hook to establish credibility.
13. Give away the exact process, including what a generic ChatGPT answer would not include.
14. Use concrete examples so readers can visualise the point.
15. Use simple words.
16. Break between ideas to make the post easy to follow, but do not add a line break after every
    line.
17. Use media to bolster the point: every image should explain or strengthen it.
18. After writing, read the post out loud ("to catch anything that sounds off").
19. Keep the post only as long as it needs to be; remove filler.
20. Make sure the body delivers everything promised in the hook.
21. Do not force a conclusion or CTA; use one only if necessary.

Caption: "There's a very specific way to write on LinkedIn. When you nail it, you can generate
serious pipeline." Tip 21 conflicts with the "one CTA matched to funnel" rule of 097 and 099; this pack
resolves it in `linkedin-post`: every post has exactly one ask, and on a reach post the ask can be
a question or a follow, never a sales line.

### Comment types [repo li-comment]

"Commenting is the highest-leverage thing on LinkedIn and the easiest to do badly. A comment on a
post with 400 reactions gets seen by more people than most of your own posts."

| # | Type | When | Shape |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Add a datum | the post makes a claim a number supports | "We saw the same thing: 40% of our..." |
| 2 | Add the missing case | right but incomplete | "This holds until {condition}. Then..." |
| 3 | Respectful disagree | genuinely wrong | name the agreement first, then the fork |
| 4 | Extend one line | one sentence is the good one | quote it, then build on it |
| 5 | Ask the real question | the post skipped the hard part | one question, specific, no "curious to hear" |
| 6 | The receipt | the commenter has done the thing | what happened, in two sentences |
| 7 | The correction | a factual error | be right, be brief, be kind, be sure |
| 8 | The reframe | right facts, wrong frame | "Another way to read this:" |
| 9 | The one-liner | presence only | under 12 words, funny or true |

Rules: 2 to 4 sentences; never open with "Great post", "Love this", "So true", "Couldn't agree
more", "This resonates", or the author's first name with an exclamation mark; no emoji openers;
never restate the post; one idea; say the specific thing; disagreement is allowed but the agreement
comes first and is real. Output two options of different types with a one-line reason. Batch mode:
5 to 10 pasted posts, one comment each, and a running note of who was commented on this week:
"Commenting on the same three people every day is visible and it looks like what it is." Never
auto-post, never use a browser to publish, never scrape; the person pastes the post text.

### Reply triage [repo li-reply]

| Bucket | What it is | What it gets |
|---|---|---|
| LEAD | someone describing the problem the poster solves | a real answer and a soft open door |
| SUBSTANCE | adds data, disagrees, extends | the longest reply on the thread |
| PEER | a name worth being seen next to | a reply that gives them something |
| SUPPORT | "great post", an emoji, a tag | a like, and a 3 to 8 word reply at most |
| NOISE | pitch, spam, bad faith | nothing, or one line and out |

Count the buckets, write in that order, stop when the value stops. Answer the actual question in the
reply (no "DM me" for an answer that fits); use their name once, at the start, never with an
exclamation mark; match their length; to a critic, concede the true part in their words first, then
hold the line, never delete, never get defensive, never reply twice on one thread; to a lead, answer
fully in public with a one-sentence open door that is an offer of help, not a pitch; ignore a pitch
in the comments (replying gives it reach). Nothing is posted until the person says yes.

### Inbox and DMs (deferred) [repo li-dm, li-inbox]

Recorded for completeness; this pack defers both (Zernio: "No (LinkedIn blocks third-party DM access)").

- **li-dm:** the 200-character invite note ({one specific reference} + {who the sender is} + {no
  ask}, with the count shown); wait a day; a first message of two to four sentences that references
  the note, gives something before asking, makes one small ask ("Worth a 15-minute call?") and has
  no calendar link; two follow-ups only: +4 days with something new (never "just bumping this"),

- **li-inbox:** sort into LEAD, RECRUITER, PEER, ASK, SPAM and print the counts first. An automated

## 13. Measurement and review

- **Rank by engagement rate and reach multiple, not impressions** [repo li-audit]. "The only honest
  source of what works for an account is that account. Every rule in every LinkedIn guide,
  including the ones in this pack, is a prior."

  | Metric | How | What it tells |
  |---|---|---|
  | Engagement rate | (reactions + comments + reposts) / impressions | whether the post earned its reach |
  | Comment ratio | comments / reactions | whether it started something |
  | Reach multiple | impressions / follower count | whether it travelled past the existing audience |
  | Save/send rate | if available | the strongest single predictor of future reach |

  "A post with 900 impressions and 40 comments beat the one with 12,000 impressions and 6." Compare
  the top 5 and bottom 5 by hook formula, format, length, theme, then day and time **last** ("It is
  almost never the cause, and it is where people want it to be"), and first-hour replies. State each
  finding with evidence and confidence; "With 30 posts you can see a pattern; with 6 you cannot".
  Output: top 5, bottom 5, what the data says, STOP, DO MORE; hand the conclusions to the plan.

  listicles got impressions and nothing else; day of week showed nothing.
- **Weekly iteration:** impressions, profile visits, best topics, formats, hooks, length and media;

### What the repo actually is [repo README]

- Eleven skills; free, MIT, no signup, no API key, nothing to connect. "Nothing gets posted until you
  say yes. These skills write. You post."
- Skills: li-post, li-comment, li-reply, li-profile, li-plan, li-human, li-carousel, li-repurpose,
  li-dm, li-inbox, li-audit. Pack mapping: li-post to `linkedin-post`; li-plan to
  `linkedin-weekly-plan`; li-comment and li-reply to `linkedin-engage`; li-profile to the profile
  mode of `linkedin-strategy`; li-audit to `linkedin-review`; li-carousel and li-repurpose stay in
  `content-atomizer`; li-human becomes the gate (written checks, plus the author's scripts as an optional local check); li-dm
  and li-inbox deferred.
- **voice.md** (template): who I am (name, what I do in one sentence, who I write for, be specific,
  what I sell); what I sound like (three own posts, words used, words never used, sentence length,
  swearing, emoji, contractions "yes - almost always yes"); three to five positions some of the
  audience does not share ("where the good posts come from"); off limits (topics, clients or numbers
  that cannot be named, claims not allowed: regulated industry, employer policy, NDA); proof
  (numbers, outcomes and stories the person will put their name on; empty means every draft carries
  `{{your number}}`). This pack stores these fields on the strategy page and in the voice profile.
- **The humaniser** (`slop.json`, counted 2026-11-13): 17 invisible-character classes, 11 typographic
  substitutions, 81 words and 32 phrases (113 lexicon entries), 11 structural tells. Automatic:
  invisible characters, typography (em dash to comma, en dash to hyphen, curly to straight quotes,
  ellipsis to three dots, bullet to hyphen), the lexicon with capitalisation preserved and URLs
  untouched. Flagged: "It's not just X, it's Y", "not only X but also Y", rule-of-three triads,
  one-word rhetorical questions, the five emoji, hashtag walls, reflex engagement bait, uniform
  sentence and bullet length. The pack's gate is in `linkedin-post`, `references/humanizer-gate.md`.
- **The five checks**, 0 to 100, higher is more human: BURSTINESS (sentence-length variation),
  SPECIFICITY (numbers, names, concrete markers per 100 words), SLOP DENSITY (lexicon hits per 100
  words), FINGERPRINT (invisible characters, em dashes, curly quotes per 1,000 characters), VOICE
  (contractions, person, structural tells). Verdict: mean weighted 60%, weakest check 40%, "because
  a detector only needs one signal to fire"; PASS needs 70+ with no check below 55. README example:
  a terrible draft scored 24.8 FLAGGED (BURSTINESS 73.0, SPECIFICITY 100.0, SLOP DENSITY 0.0 with
  19 stock terms at 24.1 per 100 words, FINGERPRINT 0.0 with 1 invisible, 1 em dash, 3 curly quotes,
  VOICE 33.3 with 3 structural tells); after `humanize.py`, 69.7 REVIEW (+44.9). "The last stretch to
  PASS is the part the script deliberately leaves to you." Two rounds is normal; five means the
  draft was written by formula and needs a different draft. Show the cleaned text and the score,
  never the score alone.
- **The honest part:** the skills do not post and should not; no official API posts to a personal
  profile without an approved partner app, and automating the site with a browser or third-party
  tool violates LinkedIn's User Agreement (https://www.linkedin.com/legal/user-agreement) and gets
  accounts restricted. "The five checks are local heuristics, not detector APIs... They are not
  GPTZero, Originality, Copyleaks, Winston or Turnitin... Nobody can honestly sell you
  'undetectable', and anybody who does is selling you something." The invisible-character pass is
  "real and it is narrow"; it is not a claim about defeating a cryptographic watermark. "Nothing
  here fabricates."
- **Pack decisions against the repo:** contractions are allowed and their absence is not penalised
  either, so the VOICE check does not score contractions; no hashtags (the repo allows
  three); no first-comment links; British spelling.

## 16. Anti-patterns

### The B2B case study, satirised [008 @dibratto 2026-08-07]

A satire of the formula, presented as what NOT to do. FlowPilot AI and Northstar Systems are
fictional. Caption: "B2B case studies all follow a very specific formula."

1. A bold headline with a big percentage: "How [company] reduced time spent on manual work by 40%".
2. "The challenge": no visibility, teams "drowning in busywork".
3. "The solution": they partnered with the vendor after evaluating multiple vendors.
4. A glowing quote from an internal champion that "your person definitely did not write".
5. Send it to the customer for a Slack thumbs-up approval and use it as a testimonial.
6. Results: "40% faster, three times more efficient, 12 hours saved per week", all "estimated by
   you".
7. Two months later the customer leaves, but they were a good logo, so it stays on the website.

**What this pack does instead:** every number comes from the facts ledger with status approved, or is
`{{your number}}`; no invented or ghost-written customer quote; no estimated results presented as
measured; no logo or case study after the customer has gone unless the ledger still approves it;
the case study tells the story (before, during, after) per 080 #1 and 098 #6.

### Other anti-patterns from the sources

- The 8am golden-hour ritual and one-line comments ("Thanks LinkedIn for another viral post",
  "congratulations on your new role, great post"). [045]
- Posting only viral content (vanity views), or only dry client-win posts. [068]
- Too much story with no proof, too much proof with no story, too much teaching with no link. [024]
- Over-optimising. [040] Using AI unless "true top 1%". [040]
- Generated comments: "generated comments are obvious and they undo the whole thing." [101]
- Generic praise comments ("Great post!"). [030, repo li-comment]
- Commenting under massive accounts where nobody sees it. [042]
- Commenting to go viral as a strategy. [042]
- A line break after every line. [079 tip 16, 017]
- Burying the lead. [079 tip 10]
- A trend post that sells. [097]
- A mistake post with no resolution. [080 #6]
- Optimising posting times before the hooks work. [repo li-plan]
- Day of week as the explanation. [repo li-audit]
- Splitting one claim across eight slides. [repo li-carousel]
- "As I mentioned in my latest video". [repo li-repurpose]
- The fifth weak post of the week. [repo li-plan]
- Engagement pods (comment accusation on 034).
- Swearing; content that ends up on r/LinkedInLunatics. [063]
- Hook traps: a hook that works with anyone's name on it; a curiosity gap never closed; the pattern
  interrupt more than once a month. [repo hooks.json]
