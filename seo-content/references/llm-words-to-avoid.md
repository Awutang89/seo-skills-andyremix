# LLM Words & Phrases to Avoid

Compiled from:
- GitHub Gist: chrisgherbert/c734ec50ae464135be57cd03b84281f9 (community-maintained rolling list)
- Blake Stockton: blakestockton.com/red-flag-words
- Embryo: embryo.com/blog/list-words-ai-overuses
- Additional cross-referencing from GPTHuman, Seotwix, WalterWrites

**Why this matters:** These words appear so frequently in LLM output that readers (and Google) pattern-match them as AI-generated. Even one or two per article erodes trust. The goal is not to ban these words forever — some are legitimate in context — but to catch them in review and replace them with more specific, natural language.

---

## How to Use This List

**During drafting:** Don't self-censor. Write freely, then run the checklist in Phase 5 (Humanize).

**During review:** Ctrl+F each word in the Phase 5 Detection Checklist section. If you find one, ask: is this the most natural word here, or did it slip in because it's statistically probable? If the latter, replace it.

**The replacement test:** Read the sentence without the flagged word. If it reads better or the same — cut it. If you need a replacement, choose the most concrete, specific alternative you can find.

## Section 1: The Core Kill List

These are the highest-frequency LLM tells. Finding even one of these in a draft is a red flag.

### Single words

| Word | Why it's a problem | Better alternative |
|---|---|---|
| delve | ChatGPT's most infamous word — use dropped in 2024 but still appears | explore, look at, get into, examine |
| leverage (verb) | Business-speak borrowed by AI as a filler verb | use, apply, take advantage of |
| utilize | Formal version of "use" that AI defaults to | use |
| foster | Vague nurturing verb AI over-applies | build, create, develop, grow |
| underscore | AI's preferred "emphasise" — rarely how humans talk | emphasise, highlight, show, prove |
| comprehensive | Signals "I covered everything" without showing it | [just describe what you covered] |
| robust | Meaningless filler adjective | strong, reliable, durable — or cut entirely |
| crucial | Everything is crucial in AI writing — nothing is | [is it actually critical? say why] |
| vital | Same problem as crucial | [explain why it matters, don't declare it] |
| essential | Same problem as crucial | [same fix] |
| paramount | Elevated synonym for crucial — AI loves it | most important, [say what comes first] |
| seamlessly | Nothing is ever not seamless in AI writing | [show how it works instead] |
| transformative | Overused to the point of meaninglessness | [describe the actual change] |
| groundbreaking | Almost never true when AI uses it | [describe what makes it new] |
| cutting-edge | Tech marketing cliché AI absorbed entirely | [describe what it does differently] |
| revolutionary | See groundbreaking | [same fix] |
| innovative | See groundbreaking | [same fix] |

## Section 2: Phrases to Kill on Sight

These phrases are so over-used they signal AI writing before the reader processes the content.

### Opening/framing phrases
- "In today's fast-paced world..."
- "In today's digital age..."
- "In today's competitive landscape..."
- "As businesses navigate the evolving landscape..."
- "As we navigate..."
- "In the world of [topic]..."
- "In the realm of [topic]..."
- "With the rise of [thing]..."
- "Whether you're a [X] or a [Y]..."
- "Are you looking for...?"
- "Look no further."
- "Without further ado..."

### Transition/connector phrases
- "It is worth noting that..."
- "It's important to note that..."
- "It's important to understand that..."
- "Important to note:"
- "That being said..."
- "With that said..."
- "At the end of the day..."
- "When it comes to..."
- "In order to..." (just say "to")
- "In terms of..."
- "Let's dive in" / "Let's dive into"
- "Let's explore"
- "Deep dive" (as a noun phrase)
- "Drill down into"
- "Take a closer look at"
- "Shed light on"
- "Fear not"
- "Remember that..."
- "Little did [they] know..."
- "Hustle and bustle"

### Filler phrases with a shorter exact equivalent

These are not stylistic preferences — each has a one-word replacement that loses nothing. AI reaches for
the long form because it is statistically common in formal text.

| Filler | Replace with |
|---|---|
| due to the fact that | because |
| in order to | to |
| in the event that | if |
| for the purpose of | to, for |
| at this point in time | now |
| in spite of the fact that | although |
| has the ability to | can |
| a majority of | most |
| in close proximity to | near |
| prior to | before |
| subsequent to | after |
| in the near future | soon |

### Conclusion/summary phrases
- "In conclusion..."
- "In summary..."
- "To summarise..."
- "Ultimately..." (as a sentence opener)
- "At the end of the day..."
- "Key insights include..."
- "Key takeaways:"
- "The bottom line is..."

### Strength/importance declarations
- "This is crucial because..."
- "This is essential for..."
- "This plays a vital role in..."
- "This is a testament to..."
- "A testament to [their excellence]..."
- "This underscores the importance of..."
- "This highlights the fact that..."

### AI metaphor phrases
- "Embark on a journey..."
- "Navigate the complexities of..."
- "Unlock the potential of..."
- "Unleash the power of..."
- "Harness the power of..."
- "Delve into the world of..."
- "Foster a culture of..."
- "A tapestry of..."
- "A symphony of..."
- "A beacon of [hope/excellence]..."
- "A treasure trove of..."
- "A unique blend of..."
- "The ever-evolving landscape of..."
- "The intersection of [X] and [Y]..."
- "The intricacies of..."

## Section 3: Transition Word Overuse

These words are not wrong — they're just overused to the point of signalling AI rhythm. Use them sparingly: one per article maximum for each.

- Furthermore
- Moreover
- Additionally
- Subsequently
- Accordingly
- Consequently
- Therefore
- Thus
- Nonetheless
- Nevertheless
- Indeed
- Certainly
- Firstly / Secondly / Thirdly (the numbered list written out in prose)
- That said

**The problem isn't the word — it's the rhythm.** AI produces these in long chains: "Furthermore... Moreover... Additionally... Subsequently..." Human writers use them once, rarely, and with intent.

## Section 5: Structure-Level AI Tells

Beyond individual words, these structural patterns signal AI writing:

**The Triple Pattern**
Everything comes in groups of three. Three benefits. Three steps. Three examples. Three bullet points per section, every section. Humans don't naturally think in threes — they think in as many points as the topic actually has.

**Perfect Parallel Bullets**
Every bullet point the same length, same grammatical structure, same level of abstraction. Real lists are messy. Some points are longer, some shorter, some more specific, some more abstract.

**The Hedge Stack**
"While X is important, it's also worth considering Y, but it depends on Z." Never commits to a position. Real expertise means taking a stance.

**The Summary Sandwich**
Intro summarises what you'll cover. Body covers it. Conclusion summarises what you covered. Treat readers like they have memory. Skip the bookends.

**Empty Transitions Between Sections**
"Now that we've covered X, let's move on to Y." Adds nothing. Cut it. Just start Y.

**Fake Objectivity**
"Some experts say X, while others believe Y." Real experts have an opinion. State it.

**Uniform Enthusiasm**
Everything is important, crucial, transformative, and insightful. If everything matters equally, nothing matters. Real writing has peaks and valleys.
