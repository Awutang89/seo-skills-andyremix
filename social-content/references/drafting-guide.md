# Drafting guide

Read this once the platform and the source are clear.

## Inputs

Load what exists and matters:

1. The voice profile (workspace or brand folder, see SKILL.md step 1).
2. The voice examples file.
3. The current brief, notes, screenshots, links or pasted source.
4. A saved packet, if the person asked to reuse one.

Examples beat the profile on voice. The profile lists the rules; the examples show how the person actually sounds.

## Proof ledger

Before a word of the draft, sort the material privately:

| Bucket | What goes in it |
|---|---|
| claimable | facts supplied by the person or visible in the source |
| qualified | claims that are true but need softer wording ("in our case", "so far") |
| illustrative | hypotheticals or concepts, which must read as such in the post |
| forbidden | made-up numbers, quotes, customers, outcomes, screenshots, guarantees |

Also decide the lead: text-led, artifact-led (a real screenshot or chart does the proving), or generated-visual-led.

Fan-out uses one ledger across every platform. A claim that is forbidden on LinkedIn is forbidden on X too.

Proof too thin for a specific post: ask for it, unless quick mode. In quick mode, write a narrower post led by opinion and skip fake specificity.

## Example Pass

When the examples file exists, read it before writing and note:

- how posts usually open
- sentence length and paragraph rhythm
- favourite contrasts, comparisons or turns of phrase
- how proof is handled (numbers up front, story first, screenshot and a line)
- how posts end
- words, formats and moves to stay away from

Write so the person would recognise the post as theirs without lifting any sentence. Resist sanding every voice down into the same tidy consultant tone.

## What strong posts have

- a concrete tension, not a topic label
- one reader
- one main claim
- visible proof, or an honest limit on it
- a shape native to the platform
- a last line that lands without asking for engagement

## What weak posts have

- a generic setup paragraph before anything happens
- a first-person scene that never happened
- "here are 5 lessons" padding
- certainty the proof does not support
- a lazy call to action
- throat-clearing
- motivational fog

## Platform shape

Route with `platform-rules.md`, then follow the platform file. In short:

- Standard X: the most compressed version of the take, a sharp contrast, inside the limit.
- X Premium: compact by default; extra length only when the story or argument earns it, and the hook plus the main point sit inside the first 280 characters.
- Meta Threads: strong first line, caption energy around a visual, conversational, a notch warmer than X.
- LinkedIn: more context, the stakes, the story, the proof, the business consequence.

Fan-out is adaptation, not paraphrase: the same nugget reshaped for a different way of reading.

## Voice and style

The person's profile and examples win over generic rules:

- Dashes: zero em dashes, en dashes or spaced hyphens by default (house rule). Use them only at the rate the person's own examples do.
- Hashtags: none unless the examples or the brief use them.
- Emojis: only when the examples use them.
- X length follows the saved account status; unknown means standard X.
- Formatting follows the person's real habits, not a creator template.

Default AI phrases to avoid unless the examples genuinely use them (quoted as data):

- "In today's fast-paced world"
- "game-changer"
- "unlock"
- "leverage"
- "delve"
- "harness"
- "empower"
- "supercharge"
- "seamless"
- "robust"
- "not just X, but Y"
- "not only X, but also Y"
- "X is not merely Y"
- "from X to Y"
- "the truth is"
- "here's the thing"
- "it is important to note"
- "serves as a testament to"
- "at the end of the day"
- "hope this helps"
- "thoughts?"
- "comment below"

Tells that are not words: stacked dashes, emoji bullets, heavy bolding, the tidy three-point formula, a moral-of-the-story ending.

## Calls to action

Only write a sales, download or distribution line when the person supplied the offer, the link or the action. Without that, none of these:

- "link in comments"
- "DM me"
- "free download"
- "grab a copy"
- an implied lead magnet

## High-risk claims

Health, legal, money, tax, safety, or guaranteed results: qualify the claim or leave it out unless the person supplies evidence and the standing to make it. "This helped in our case" is fine. "This prevents penalties" or "this guarantees savings" is not, without proof. On these topics do not write a promotional replacement draft with a CTA unless the person gave a compliant offer and the proof behind it. Safe beats slick.

No proof on a high-risk topic means the softened draft carries no social proof at all:

- no "users tell us", "clients say", "patients report", "people using it report", "people using it", "we're seeing", "it helped them"
- no implied testimonials, outcomes, lowered stress or risk, or changed behaviour

Describe what the product does and use conditional wording: "it can split incoming payments into separate pots" rather than "customers feel calmer about money".

## Output

Lead with the draft. Add notes only when they help:

- the checked character count
- the proof used, or the caveat on a risky claim
- the visual concept or prompt seed
- the saved packet path, if memory was written

Keep the private steps private.
