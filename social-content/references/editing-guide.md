# Editing guide

Read this once a first draft exists.

## Default pass

Three checks before anything is shown:

1. Hook and platform fit.
2. Proof and specificity.
3. Voice match and AI cleanup.

For quick posts and one-offs, that is enough. Then run the lint:

```text
python "<repo>/social-content/scripts/check_post.py" post --platform <x|x-premium|threads|linkedin> --file draft.txt [--voice-file <voice-examples.md>]
```

When a packet is saved, its Editing Pass section records what was really checked or changed: two to four specific notes covering hook and platform fit, proof or qualification, voice and AI cleanup, and any change made because of the visual. It is evidence, not a ritual checkbox.

## 1. Hook and platform fit

- Does line one create tension, show proof, or open a specific scene?
- Is the requested platform the only one delivered, and is every requested platform there?
- Does the shape suit the platform?
- Is it easy to scan without feeling like a template?

Weak openings to replace:

- "I wanted to share..."
- "In today's world..."
- "Here are my thoughts on..."
- "AI is changing everything..."

Better: a concrete before and after, a real objection, or a pointed line lifted from the person's own material.

## 2. Proof and specificity

For every specific claim:

- Did the person supply it, is it visible in the source, or is it plainly hypothetical?
- Is each number attached to the right entity (their store, not the industry; this month, not all time)?
- Are legal, medical, money and performance claims qualified?
- Did a client, quote, metric, screenshot or outcome sneak in that nobody supplied?
- Did social proof ("users tell us", "clients say", "we're seeing", "people report") appear without a source?

Thin proof: make the claim narrower. Never dress it up with an anecdote that did not happen.

High-risk topics (legal, medical, money, tax, safety, performance): no testimonials, behaviour claims, download CTAs, implied lead magnets or implied results unless supplied. With no proof at all, strip every social proof phrase ("users tell us", "clients say", "patients report", "people using it", "we're seeing", "it helped them") and replace it with a plain sentence about how the product works, or ask for the evidence.

## 3. Voice and AI cleanup

Hold the draft against the profile and the examples: sentence length, paragraph rhythm, how blunt or warm, favourite words and moves, banned words and formats, endings.

This is a taste pass, not a blanket ban list. Keep any pattern the person's real examples use; rewrite patterns that make the post read like default model output.

Default rewrite targets unless the examples show the person likes them:

- Stock verbs: "unlock", "leverage", "delve", "harness", "empower", "supercharge", "elevate", "transform". Use a plainer, exact verb.
- Openers that say nothing: "In today's world", "In an ever-evolving landscape", "I wanted to share", "Let's dive in", "Here's the thing", "The truth is".
- Symmetric templates: "not only X but also Y", "X isn't just Y, it's Z", "from X to Y", "whether you're X or Y", and suspiciously neat lists of three.
- Empty drama frames: "but that's only half the story", "what happened next surprised me", "this changed everything", unless the source earns the drama.
- Borrowed authority: "it is important to note", "it is worth mentioning", "needless to say", "in conclusion", "serves as a testament to".
- Hype adjectives: "game-changing", "cutting-edge", "revolutionary", "seamless", "robust", "transformative", "powerful", "impactful", "unprecedented", unless literally true or quoted.
- Moral endings: "the lesson is", "the key takeaway", "at the end of the day", "hope this helps". Replace with the specific consequence from this post.
- Lazy CTA: "thoughts?", "agree?", "comment below", "drop a comment", "follow for more", unless the person explicitly wants engagement bait.
- Distribution crutches: "link in comments", "DM me", "grab a copy", "free download" and cousins, unless the person supplied the offer and asked for promotion.
- Over-smoothing: when the draft is too balanced, put back one real friction point, caveat, odd detail or sharper opinion.

Punctuation and format:

- Dashes: the house default is zero in the copy. If the person's examples use dashes, match their rate; two or more in a short post is still worth a second look.
- No emoji bullets or decorative emoji stacks unless the examples use them.
- No heavy bolding, runs of one-line paragraphs, or the "hook, three bullets, lesson, CTA" skeleton unless that is genuinely how the person writes.
- Hashtags depend on platform and voice. Delete hashtag clouds unless the examples show them.

Structural tell scan before presenting:

- Would the post work for any industry by swapping one noun?
- Is it a safe, balanced point with nothing at stake?
- Does it explain the obvious like a classroom handout?
- Does a generic anecdote stand where the person's own proof should be?
- Does the ending ask for engagement instead of landing a point?

## Deeper pass

For high-stakes posts, long posts, real-voice emulation, or a weak first draft:

- write two genuinely different versions
- keep the stronger one
- cut or rewrite its weakest sentence
- tighten the paragraph order
- check the last line actually lands

The note about this stays short. The person wants a better post, not a lab report.

## Final bar

A post ships only if it is:

- specific enough that a generic writer could not have produced it from the topic alone
- honest about what is proven and what is not
- recognisably shaped by the person's voice evidence
- native to the requested platform
- free of lazy CTAs and obvious AI filler
- free of any unsupported visual or image-generation claim
- free of any claim that an edit, rewrite or reference read happened when it did not
