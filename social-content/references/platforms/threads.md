# Meta Threads

Meta Threads is not an X thread. Keep the two apart in every draft and label.

Threads sits nearer X than LinkedIn, with a warmer edge: sharp first lines and conversational delivery, less dunking, less aggression, more "let's think this through together".

## Platform facts (re-check; they change)

- A standard post holds up to 500 characters.
- Posts can carry links, photos and video.
- A multi-post thread is built by adding posts under the first one.
- Topics (tags) exist; treat one as a single useful label, not a hashtag strategy.
- Long text attachments exist but are not the default for everyday posts.

## Best fit

- captions that ride on a visual
- conversational observations
- short sequences that scan easily
- reading a screenshot or image for the viewer
- warmer, less formal posts
- quick personal takes that still make a point
- observations with a few beats that would feel cramped in one 500-character post

## Hard limits

- Under 500 characters per post unless a longer sequence was requested.
- Threads only when Threads was requested; never produce an X thread by accident.
- At most one Topic, and only when the voice or a discovery goal supports it.
- No hashtag clouds or engagement-hack formatting.

## Shape

- Natural, made for the scroll.
- Anchored on the visual, the proof or the scene when there is one.
- A strong first line.
- Light paragraphs or a compact sequence.
- Interprets a screenshot instead of describing it.
- Conversational, never vague.
- Friendlier than X, still with a point.
- No performative outrage unless the person really writes that way.

## First lines

The opening should create one of: a concrete tension, a useful surprise, a specific piece of proof, a recognisable observation, or a visual reason to stop. It should sound like something a person would say out loud, not a headline straining for clicks.

Avoid:

- "Here's the thing"
- "Unpopular opinion"
- "Nobody talks about..."
- "This changed everything"
- a generic question with nothing behind it

## With a visual

- Caption what it means, not every pixel.
- Keep sourced numbers exact.
- Add no context that is neither visible nor supplied.
- Image and caption work as a pair; neither repeats the other.

## Multi-post Threads

Use a sequence when the idea has stages, proof, a contrast, or a short story that would suffer crushed into one post.

A good sequence:

- opens with a first post that stands alone
- keeps every post under 500 characters
- makes each reply add a new beat, never a sentence fragment
- lands the point in the final post instead of asking for replies
- stays friendly and direct

Choose a sequence when the person asked for one, the source has two to five clear beats, the post needs proof plus interpretation, or a screenshot needs one post for context and another for the takeaway.

Choose a single post when the idea fits under 500, the proof is thin, or a sequence would only chop one thought into bits.

Separate posts with a line containing only `---`.

## Topics

- One relevant Topic at most.
- Never a hashtag cloud.
- Never a Topic to prop up vague copy.
- None when the person's voice avoids them.

## Failure modes

- a LinkedIn post with shorter lines
- every request turned into a multi-post thread
- describing the visual instead of interpreting it
- cosy commentary with no point
- borrowing X's aggression instead of softening it
- a weak first line that hides the reason to read
- Topics used like Instagram hashtags
